★ Case Studies

The proof,
build by build.

The challenge, what the system took over, and what changed. The same doctrine every time: name the leak, systemize it, run it.

Representative engagements from our two founders' combined build library, with details anonymized to protect the businesses involved.

Field Service · HVAC

The dispatch board lived in the owner's head, and the owner wanted his evenings back.

The Challenge

Every morning began with an hour of manual scheduling across crews, and every change rippled through the day by phone call. Jobs slipped, techs idled between sites, and nobody but the owner could run the board.

What We Built

One system that holds the whole job pipeline: intake, scheduling, dispatch, and the follow up. It proposes each day's board from the rules the owner already carried in his head; a person reviews and approves it in minutes.

What Changed

The morning hour became minutes, schedule changes stopped requiring the owner, and the board finally survived a vacation. The system now runs follow ups the office used to forget.

Trades · Plumbing

The work was profitable. The cash just arrived six weeks late.

The Challenge

Invoices went out days after jobs closed, and nobody owned the chase. Receivables drifted while everyone stayed busy on the next job, and the owner floated payroll from a personal line more than once.

What We Built

Invoicing wired directly to job completion, with a polite, persistent chase the system runs on its own schedule: reminders, statements, and escalation to a human at exactly the right moment.

What Changed

Invoices out the day the job closes. The chase happens every time, without anyone remembering it. The gap between work done and cash in hand shrank from weeks toward days, and stayed there.

Industrial · Manufacturing

Every month closed with a spreadsheet scramble and a report nobody trusted.

The Challenge

Job costs lived in three tools and two heads. By the time the numbers were assembled, the month was over and the margin leaks it revealed had already repeated themselves.

What We Built

One reporting spine over jobs, costs, and hours, fed continuously instead of assembled monthly. The system flags margin drift while the job is still running, not after it ships.

What Changed

The owner sees this week's numbers this week. Underpriced work gets caught mid job. The month end scramble became a report the system writes itself.

Local Service · Fire Protection

Compliance deadlines ran the business, and the business ran on memory.

The Challenge

Inspections, certifications, and renewal dates lived in a wall calendar and a veteran employee's memory. A missed date meant a lost contract, and the veteran was talking about retiring.

What We Built

The system took custody of every deadline, every site, and every requirement, then took over the scheduling and customer notices that surround them. The veteran's knowledge was written into the system while he was still there to check it.

What Changed

No date depends on memory. Customers get notified before they think to ask, renewals arrive signed, and the business survived the retirement it was quietly afraid of.

Construction · General Contracting

The back office grew faster than the crews it existed to support.

The Challenge

Each new project added coordination: submittals, change orders, vendor chases, status calls. Office headcount climbed while field productivity stayed flat, and margins thinned as the company grew.

What We Built

A system that owns the paper trail: documents filed, vendors chased, status reported, change orders tracked from request to signature, with people approving the moments that matter.

What Changed

The company took on more work without adding office payroll. Coordination stopped being a job title. Growth started showing up in margin again, which is where it belongs.
